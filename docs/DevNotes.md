- Bug: creatures, rivals and town NPCs (and caches, shrine, gates, beacons) all rendered plain grey instead of their region/rival colours. Checked BasicShapeMaterial over the Unreal MCP: its colour parameter is still called "Color" in 5.9, so the name was fine. Real cause: the /Engine/BasicShapes meshes carry the plain DefaultMaterial (no Color param), not BasicShapeMaterial, so CreateDynamicMaterialInstance(0) was making a dynamic instance of DefaultMaterial and SetVectorParameterValue("Color") silently did nothing. Fix: pass BasicShapeMaterial as the source material to CreateDynamicMaterialInstance(0, mat) in each actor (loaded at runtime - a constructor FObjectFinder for it fails at module init, though the mesh finders next to it succeed). Added +DirectoriesToAlwaysCook=(Path="/Engine/BasicShapes") so the material ships in the packaged build. ## Fri 13 Nov, late: T3, slicing and gore
- T3 chunk 1: the plushie body is no longer the engine's sphere. It is a UV sphere generated in C++ (32 around, 16 top to bottom) inside a ProceduralMeshComponent, at its real size, so it can be sliced at the snip and works in a packaged build (the engine sphere can't be copied there: no CPU access). UVs run once around with the seam at the back and top to bottom, with true ellipsoid normals and tangents, ready for the plush fabric texture in T4/T7. Ears, eyes and stitches are still attached basic shapes. In the game it looks the same as before.
- UV check: made a temporary grid material (8 x 4 tiles, red = u, green = v) over the MCP, put it on the bodies in Play and captured close-ups: even squares at the equator, one seam down the back, nothing mirrored, a clean fan at the poles. The material was deleted afterwards. Screenshots for the log: Saved/Screenshots/KP/T3_1_uv_front.png and T3_1_uv_back.png (the seam), T3_1_procedural_bodies.png (the game view, unchanged).
- T3 chunk 2: real slicing at the snip. SliceProceduralMesh cuts each marked body along a vertical plane through its centre (normal = cut direction x up), with a red cap as a new mesh section. Ears, eyes and stitches re-attach to whichever half they sit on. Each half gets a small convex hull (about 30 points, built from the known shape instead of sliced from collision), simulates physics and is pushed apart at the DESIGN.md speeds, with the toy's drag as linear damping. Halves live 6 game seconds, then shrink away; at most 60 pieces, the oldest shrink first.
- Bug: after the first working slice no red was visible from the game camera. A paused close-up showed why: the halves were tipping onto their cut faces, red side down, and rolling about 400 units away like balls. The tip-over spin had the wrong sign (it fought gravity), and nothing matched the toy's drag. Fix: spin the top of each half away from the cut (velocity = spin x radius) and add linear damping 2.8 (the toy's 0.06 per second). Now they land like bowls, red side up, 90 to 200 units from the cut. Screenshot pair for the log: Saved/Screenshots/KP/T3_2_cap_closeup_a.png (before: dark cap facing the mat) and T3_2_cap_closeup.png (after: two red caps facing the camera), plus T3_2_slice_settled.png (game view).
- Performance: one slice costs about 2 ms (slice, two hull cooks, re-attaching 14 parts). A six-plushie cut took 10 ms in one frame, so the snip now slices at most 3 plushies per frame and the rest follow on the next frames; score and combo text still happen at once. The log prints "Snip: sliced N in X ms".
- Test tool: kp.AutoCut 1 (or -KPAutoCut on the command line, with -KPGod, -KPWave=N, -KPShots=N) makes her cut through the nearest plushies by herself and save screenshots after snips. Built so a packaged build can be checked without anyone playing it; also handy for soak tests. Before that, cuts were tested by driving the real mouse through the Slate inspector over MCP.
- Piece cap tested by setting maxPieces to 8 in the JSON: the count held at 4 cut plushies (8 halves), briefly 5 to 6 while the oldest shrank.

- T3b, making the halves read as cut plushies (11:00 to 12:00). New cap material M_KPCap, built over the MCP with editor Python. Trick: every point on a cap's rim lies on the body's surface, so each pixel's distance from the body centre, measured in body radii, is exactly 1 at the rim for any cut, centred or not. That gives bands from the rim inwards without using the cap UVs: a thin band of the plushie's own pastel skin, a pale pink stuffing band, then glossy wet red with darker noise blotches. The Theatrical version keeps the skin band and fills the rest with lumpy white stuffing, with a little self-light (through EyeAdaptationInverse) so it reads white at night without looking unlit. Each cut plushie now gets its own material instance (its radius and colour), shared by its two halves; kp.Cut still flips halves already on the mat.
- How the halves land: a half dome always rocks onto its round side, so before this they all ended up cut side up (flat discs from the camera). Now a share of them (halfCapUpShare in the JSON, 0.33) are still tipped cut side up; for the rest the centre of mass is moved to the middle of the cut face, which makes the dome balance at any tilt, so they stay wherever tumbling leaves them. Measured over the MCP, about 28 halves each: share 1.0 gave 23 up, 1 sideways; share 0.33 gave 13 up, 11 sideways, 4 face down; share 0 gave 1 up, 19 sideways, 6 face down. Some "natural" halves still roll onto their backs, so 0.33 lands about half of them cut side up.
